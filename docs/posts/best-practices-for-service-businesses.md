# Best Practices for Service-Based Businesses

Running a service-based business means your calendar IS your revenue. Every no-show is money lost, every double-booking is a trust broken. BookIt gives you the tools — but how you use them decides how much you grow. Here are the practices that separate thriving service businesses from the rest.

## 1. Design Services Around Real Time

Don't just set a duration — think about what happens before and after each appointment.

- **Add buffer time** between bookings. A consultation that runs 5 minutes late snowballs into a day of chaos. A 10-minute buffer absorbs it.
- **Separate prep from delivery.** If you need time to review a client's materials before a call, that's a different service slot than the call itself.
- **Block personal time.** Your lunch break, deep-work hours, and days off should be visible on the calendar as unavailable — before a client ever books.

## 2. Collect the Right Data at Booking Time

The booking form is your first handshake with a customer. Ask for what you need to deliver, not what feels nice to have.

- **Custom fields per service.** A fitness coach needs skill level and goals; a salon needs hair type and color history. Collect them at booking so you're never scrambling before the session.
- **Keep forms short.** Every extra field is a drop-off risk. Two or three questions max, unless the service genuinely requires more.
- **Require contact info you'll actually use.** Confirmations are useless if the email has a typo.

## 3. Automate Reminders — Then Check Them

Reminders are the highest-ROI feature in any booking system.

- **Send two reminders.** One 24 hours out, one 2-3 hours before. That second one is what catches the "I forgot" crowd.
- **Make cancellation easy.** A client who can cancel in one click will do it early — and you can refill the slot. A client who can't cancel will just no-show.
- **Track your no-show rate.** If it stays above 5-8%, tighten your confirmation flow or add a small deposit requirement.

## 4. Create a Buffer for No-Shows

No-shows still happen. Build resilience:

- **Deposits for high-demand services.** A small upfront payment filters out casual bookers.
- **Waitlists.** When a slot frees up, your most eager customers fill it within minutes.
- **Overlap strategy.** For classes with capacity, overbook by one or two seats based on historical no-show rates — carefully.

## 5. Keep Your Calendar Honest

- **Update availability when plans change.** A stale calendar is worse than no calendar — it burns client trust.
- **Review booking patterns monthly.** Which times never book? Kill them. Which days overflow? Add capacity.
- **Let staff own their schedules.** Give each team member their own availability and service list. Centralized control is slower and creates resentment.

## 6. Use Reports to Run the Business, Not Just the Calendar

Your booking data is a goldmine:

- **Revenue per service.** Find your best margins and push them.
- **Utilization rate.** Are you 60% booked on a service you could compress into 3 days a week?
- **Peak hours.** Staff your strongest sellers at your busiest times.

## 7. Own Your Data — Don't Rent It

- **No data mining** of your customer list by a third-party SaaS.

You built your business. The system that runs it should belong to you.
